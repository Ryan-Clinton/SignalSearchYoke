## The search

- **Data:** the finest product (`.gpuspec.0000.h5`): 2.794 Hz channels, 16 spectra of 18.25 s (274 s between the first and the last).
- **Drifts:** ±4 Hz/s in steps of 0.0102 Hz/s, each line summed with a box of channels as wide as its smear (docs/seti.md, "A correct search").
- **SNR:** the excess along a line over the robust spread of the summed lines themselves (true noise of the sums; turboSETI's SNR is about 0.23 of it). The cut: SNR 10; shadow hits from 5 kept for the OFF test and the checks.
- **The cadence test** (`onoff.py`): every ON look's hits seed events; hits join by one distance, one to one, every member matching every other; every look measured along the track. Interference: an OFF look at S/N 6 or more along the track, or any hit there. Candidate: in every ON look (a hit, or S/N 5 along the track), no OFF evidence.
- **The checks** (`vetting.py`, after Sheikh et al. 2021's ten steps for blc1): data sanity, the OFF looks, known bands, zero drift, the Earth's drift, recurrence, look-alikes, harmonics and intermodulation, crowding, the report; each marked shown, indicated or coincidence possible with its chance. None deletes a candidate.

## What this search can and cannot see

The completeness and the limits below hold for this signal and no other:

- **A tone narrower than one channel** (2.79 Hz) at the transmitter; wider signals spread over channels and lose sensitivity, not modelled (Voyager 1's 322 Hz sidebands came out as dozens of hits).
- **Drifting steadily**, within ±4 Hz/s (steps of 0.0102 Hz/s), as the planted signals did. A drift that changes by more than about 2.6e-04 Hz/s² leaves its line by half a channel within a look (an estimate, not measured: comparable.md step 9).
- **On through each ON look** (16 spectra of 18.25 s, 292 s): a signal on for part of a look gives about that share of its power (a pulse train shorter than 18 s is averaged into a steady one at its mean power).
- **In all three ON looks** (a hit at SNR 10 in one, at least S/N 5 along the track in the others), **and in none of the three OFF looks** (under S/N 6 along the track and no hit there): a transmitter also seen in the OFF directions is called interference by design; one on in some ON looks only is unconfirmed.
- **Seen in total power** (both polarisations summed): any polarisation.
- **With the channel response measured on Voyager 1's carrier** (`measure.py response`): rectangular (no window, sinc^2) fits best, by χ² 1949.4 over the next (χ²/dof 3.721), the response the planted signals used. Not a perfect fit: residuals up to 0.5% of the peak, and the far wings above sinc² (at 10 to 20 channels 0.0017 of the peak against 0.0005), which the carrier's own phase noise could give. One observation, of September 2016; other sessions' processing is taken to be the same, not shown to be.

It cannot see:

- drifts beyond ±4 Hz/s (the `.0002` product could reach about 2.7 kHz/s: comparable.md step 9);
- signals wider than a few channels, broadband or dispersed pulses, and pulses resolved in time (the finest time here is one spectrum);
- the receiver's notch (60.0 MHz searched there) and the blocks where a look stopped at the 2,000-hit limit (41.0 MHz), left out of the limits;
- line starts within about 422 channels (1.2 kHz) of each coarse channel's edges, where a line would run off the block (about 0.08% of the band), and the blanked middle channel of each;
- anything outside the half-hour of the cadence or the beam (about 5.8′ wide at 2.3 GHz, centred on the star).

**Dropped data** (`measure.py dropped`, from the files' chunk indexes): a test for spectra of zeros, which compress to almost nothing, found 0 in any coarse channel of the six looks, and 0 missing or dropped nodes (no `zz` or `oo` in the names); the smallest chunk is 97% of its file's median. This does not show that every sample is intact: packets lost within a spectrum lower its power without zeroing it, and are not seen this way.

## Validation

- **Voyager 1** (`measure.py voyager`, SHA-256 c9a9a54f4140…): the carrier and both sidebands found within one channel and two drift steps of turboSETI's published hits; drift -0.3776 Hz/s against the Earth's -0.3772. Its 322 Hz-wide sidebands are tiled with many hits: wide signals are outside this search.
- **Price et al. 2020, AJ 159, 86**, HIP 91699 at 2200.0 to 2200.6 MHz (`measure.py price`): their pipeline run here finds their S-band event; this search finds it as two candidates (kept as two) and the checks mark both likely interference, as they judged by eye. Their hit lists are not published, so no star's hits could be set against theirs.
- **turboSETI** on seven real looks from two sessions: outside one burst of interference it found nothing this search missed (docs/seti.md, "Checked against turboSETI").
- **Not done:** the 2017 cadence's own SEFD (the one measured is from 2019); the coarse polyphase filterbank's roll-off; non-steady signals (pulsed, chirped, wide); re-observation (no telescope here).
